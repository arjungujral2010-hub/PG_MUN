Planetary Stewardship Forum

United Nations Environment Programme

Committee Overview & Introduction

The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) is the global authority setting the environmental agenda, promoting the coherent implementation of the environmental dimension of sustainable development within the UN system, and serving as an authoritative advocate for the global environment.

At PG-MUN 2026, the UNEP committee invites delegates to confront interconnected ecological crises: climate volatility, loss of biodiversity, oceanic ecosystem degradation, and resource depletion. Delegates will grapple with the urgent balance between economic industrial development and ecological conservation, drafting multilateral frameworks that ensure climate equity and sustainable governance for future generations.

Official Agenda Coming Soon

The substantive agenda for the United Nations Environment Programme is in the final stages of formulation by the Secretariat to deliver a rigorous, scientifically grounded debate on planetary challenges.

Background Guide & Environmental Dossier

The UNEP study guide provides delegates with foundational scientific literature, historical context of multilateral environmental treaties (such as the Paris Agreement and Rio Declarations), treaty frameworks, and key questions to guide national policy drafting.

Delegate Preparation Guidelines

National Environmental Data: Examine your country's carbon footprint, renewable energy matrix, climate vulnerability index, and biodiversity laws.

Treaty Commitments: Review Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), SDG 13, 14 & 15, and commitments under international protocols.

Climate Finance & Technology: Build coalitions around tech transfer, Green Climate Fund allocations, and equitable transition pathways.

Actionable Drafts: Focus on feasible, legally sound resolutions that balance environmental urgency with economic sustainability.
